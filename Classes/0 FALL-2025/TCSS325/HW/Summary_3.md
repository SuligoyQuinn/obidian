| Virtue Theory        | There are virtuous people that we all strive to be, and by focusing on being them the right actions will follow. Aristotle believes that humans are as good as they can fulfill their functions, both as normal animals and as rational animals. These virtues that nature has set as humanity's function are midpoints between two vices. | Virtues are extremely vague and seem unachievable. The only way to become a virtuous person is to learn from someone who has already become virtuous. Aristotle claims that the only way to live a life of eudaimonia is to achieve these virtues. |
| Natural Law Theory   | God created the world according to natural laws so that life is sustained. Through this he instilled in creatures the want for things that are good for them. Human instincts show us these good things and our reasoning allows us to find the natural law in them.                                                                       | If these natural laws are so universal and intuitive how do so many people break the natural law? Furthermore just because some of these natural laws can be observed, are they inherently good, and should we follow them?                        |
| Justice Theory       | An ethically correct decision is a fair decision that results in equality. All decisions should distribute to people equally and not favor those that do not need it.                                                                                                                                                                      | Justice has many different definitions to different people. How can we make a unified theory if everyone disagrees on the core value of it?                                                                                                        |
| Feminist Care Ethics | Impartiality is not realistic we value some people over others, and it is important to listen to those emotions. This care for others  ought to be the basis of our ethical theorizing, not just critical thought.                                                                                                                         | The existence of situations where impartiality is not feasible does not mean it is never needed. Bias in general is a core problem of moral thinking, and needs to be eliminated on both sides.                                                    |
| Ethical Egoism       | It is morally right to do what is actually best for you. Because everyone acts in their own self interest, it is acceptable and ethically correct to prioritize yourself.                                                                                                                                                                  | Ethics should prevent you from doing whatever you want and instead make you work for the good of society. There is a thin line between acting in self interest and selfishness.                                                                    |
| Biology of Morality  | The value of taking care of ones self in embedded in our biology. The evolution in mammals to take care of young extends this value to the young as well. Eventually as homeo sapains evolve this extends to groups as well.                                                                                                               |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
