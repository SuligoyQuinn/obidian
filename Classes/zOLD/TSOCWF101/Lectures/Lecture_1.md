# Ethics
## Ethical Absolutism
- Fixed rules that guide decisions
- Don't deviate from rules
- "A social worker shall respect a client's right to self-determination"
- Ethical rules supersede potential consequences
## Ethical Relativism
- Reject non compromising guidelines
- Weigh potential risks and outcomes
- Decisions are informed by situational context
- Outcome based decision making
## NASW Values 
- Service
	- Primary goal is to help people in need and address social problems
- Social Justice
	- Challenge social injustice
	- Pursue social change on behalf of vulnerable and oppressed individuals
- Dignity and Worth of the Person
	- Respect the inherent dignity and worth of the person
	- treat each person in a caring and respectful fashion
- Importance of human relationships
	- Recognize central importance of human relationships
	- engage people as partners in the helping process
	- relationships are a vehicle for change
- Integrity
	- Behave in a trustworthy manner
- Competence
	- Practice within areas of expertise
## Principles of Bioethics
- Beneficence
	- To do good
- Non-maleficence
	- Do not harm
- Questions raised
	- Does my perception of what is best for the patient differ from the clients perception
