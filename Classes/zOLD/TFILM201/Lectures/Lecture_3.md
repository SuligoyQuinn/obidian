# Logic of Genres: Industrial Model
- Common language for critics, producers, directors, exhibitors, audiences
	- Structurally, Visually similar to each other
- Product Identity
	- inform customers
	- instant recognition
		- can figure out movie from poster
- Create Narrative shorthand
	- Tell a story with less backstory
	- Rely on audience knowledge and skill
- Allow for variation within a formula
- Diversify product line
	- aim to different general audiences
# What makes a Genre
- A *genre* of texts that share a set of narrative, stylistic, and thematic rules or conventions
- Narrative conventions
	- subject matter, narrative form, plot, and characters
- Genre and Film style
	- visual and sound conventions
	- iconography of a genre

# Stages of Film Genres
- Experimental stage
- Classical stage
	- Traditional genre films
- Refinement/Revisionist stage
	- formal, stylistic, and thematic details are problematizes. Complex characters. Typical expectations of the genre are subverted
- Parodic/Baroque Stage

## Cultural Context and Thematic Myths
- Contain ideas that celebrate individual heroism
- Foundational tenets of U.S. national mythology
- Lone hero is typically a "chosen one" every-person archetype who fights for the personal interests and/or those of the family, community or nation
- Hero must succeed by taking violent action
- Martyrdom and Sacrifice
- Affirmation of the right to bear arms to protect community
# Two Basic Types/Categories of Film Genres
## Type 1: Social Order
- Action, traditionally male-dominated genres
- Superhero, Western, Gangster, Sci-fi, Detective, Adventure, War
- Action
	- violence needed to obtain goals
- Space
	- Public, unstable, dangerous
- Hero
	- Male loner or buddies on a quest, free to enter social space of others
- Conflicts
	- Good vs Evil, threats to peace
- Resolution
	- Reestablish social order through violence
## Type 2: Domestic Order
- pro-social, focus on social relationships and social conflicts
- female-dominated genres
- Melodrama, Musical, Rom-com, Comedy, Horror, thriller
- Emotion rather than action
	- social relationships
- Space
	- private/domestic
- Hero
	- couple or individual - face obstacles or threats to social order
- Conflicts
	- bound by social norms and values, transgressive; forbidden love
