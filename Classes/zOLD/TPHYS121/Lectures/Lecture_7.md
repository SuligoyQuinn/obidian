## Rotational Motion
- In translational motion, orientation of the object stays the same
- In rotational motion orientation of an object rotates around a fixed point
- In real life rigid objects tend to combine both motions
### Uniform Circular Motion
- In circular motion, the object travels in a circle and the velocity is tangent to the circle at every instant
- Acceleration has constant magnitude but varying direction
- velocity and acceleration are always perpendicular
- Even if object is moving at constant speed, velocity changes direction, meaning there is acceleration
### Centripetal Acceleration ($\vec{a}_{c}$)
- Making a sharp turn in a car
	- If you speed up it is harder
		- $a_{c}\propto v$
	- Taking a wider turn is easier
		- $a_{c}\propto \frac{1}{r}$
	- Units are wrong with those equations
- $a_{c}=\frac{v^2}{r}$
### Period(T) and Frequency(f)
- The time it takes to complete one revolution is T
- Number of revolutions per second is called frequency
	- $f=\frac{1}{T}$
	- Unit: Hz
$$v=\frac{distance}{time}=\frac{1 circumfrance}{1period}=\frac{2\pi r}{T}$$
### Measuring Rotation
Rotational Position: $\theta$
Rotational Velocity: $\omega$
Rotational Acceleration: $\alpha$
### Position
$$
\theta=\frac{arclength}{radius}=\frac{s}{r}
$$
- $\theta$ is measured in radians (rad)
- Radians are unitless
- Conversion factor: $\pi rad=180$
### Velocity
$$
\omega _{avg}=\frac{\nabla \theta}{\nabla t}
$$
- Measured in radians over time
$$
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}=2\pi f
$$
- Angular frequency for constant angular speed
- Things turn fast sometimes use revolutions per minute
- $1rev=2\pi rad$
#### Direction of Velocity
1. Wrap fingers in direction of the rotation
2. Thumb is pointing in direction of $\omega$
### Acceleration
$$
\alpha=\frac{\nabla \omega}{\nabla t}
$$
- Units are $\frac{rad}{\sec ^2}$

### Constant Angular Acceleration Motion
Same equations as translational motion
$$
\theta_{f}=\theta_{i}+\omega_{i}\nabla t+\frac{1}{2}\alpha (\nabla t)^2
$$
$$
\omega_{f}=\omega_{i}+\alpha \nabla t
$$
$$
(\omega_{f})^2=(\omega_{i})^2+2\alpha \nabla \theta
$$
### Tangential speed ($v$)  vs Angular Speed ($\omega$)
- Each point on the object has an instantaneous linear velocity that is tangential to the circle of rotation at that radius
$$
v=\frac{\nabla s}{\nabla t}=\frac{\nabla(r\theta)}{\nabla t}=r (\frac{\nabla \theta}{\nabla t})=r\omega
$$
