## **Constant Velocity**
- motion diagrams tell us only where an object is at a few points
- position vs time graph tell us position at all times
- graphs are an abstract representation of motion
### $x(t)$ Graph
- in an $x(t)$ graph velocity is slope of the graph
- sign of slope represents direction
- steepness of slope is speed
$$
x(t)=x_{i}+vt
$$
### $v(t)$ Graph
- Area under the curve gives displacement
$$
\nabla x=V_{Avg}\nabla t
$$
## **Instantaneous Velocity**
- For particular instance in time, not average
- Like a car speedometer
$$
v_{x}=\lim_{ \nabla t \to 0 } \frac{\nabla x}{\nabla t}=\frac{dx}{dt}
$$
- Mathematically is the derivative of position function
## **Acceleration**
- Acceleration describes rate of change of velocity
$$
\vec{a}_{Avg}=\frac{\vec{\nabla}v}{\nabla t}
$$
- SI units: $m/s^2$
- meters per second per second
- If acceleration vector points in same direction as velocity vector: object is speeding up
- points in opposite direction: object is slowing down
- If 0: object is moving with constant velocity
## **Motion in 1-dimension**
### Sign of Position, Velocity, and Acceleration
- $x>0$ when object is on positive side of the origin
- $v_{x}>0$ when velocity vector points to the positive direction
- $a_{x}>0$ when acceleration vector points to the positive direction
- doesn't mean object is speeding up
## **Acceleration from a $v(t)$ graph**
- slope of the line tangent to a point gives instantaneous acceleration
