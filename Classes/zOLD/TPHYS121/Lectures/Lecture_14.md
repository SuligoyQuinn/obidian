## Totally Inelastic collision
- Collision in which the two objects stick together and move with a common final velocity
Examples:
- A piece of clay hits the floor
- A dart hitting a dart board
Momentum is conserved
$$
p_{1i}+p_{2i}=p_{1f}+p_{2f}
$$
$$
p=mv
$$
Kinetic Energy is not conserved
$$
K_{1i}+K_{2i}>K_{1f}+K_{2f}
$$
$$
K=\frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$
## Internal Energy ($E_{int}$)
- Internal energy is associated with the state of the object
- In collisions, the deformation of the objects changes the internal energy of the object (ex thermal energy raising the temperature of the object)

| State Change     | Internal Energy |
| ---------------- | --------------- |
| temperature      | thermal         |
| chemical         | chemical        |
| reversable shape | elastic         |
| phase            | transformation  |
## Conservation of Energy in Collision
- Total energy of a system is the sum of the kinetic energy and internal energy of the system
- total energy of a system of two colliding objects does not change
- The internal energy of the objects is increased by how much their kinetic energy decreases
## Inelastic collisions
- Relative speeds after the collision is less than before the collision
$$
|\nabla v_{sys i}|>|\nabla v_{sysf}|
$$
- total momentum is conserved in an isolated system
$$
p_{1i}+p_{2i}=p_{1f}+p_{2f}
$$
- Kinetic energy is not conserved
$$
K_{f}<K_{i}
$$
## Elastic collisions
- Collisions between two very hard objects
- During collision, energy is stored in compressed bonds and then released
- kinetic energy of the system does not change
$$
v_{2i}-v_{1i}=-(v_{2f}-v_{1f})
$$
- total momentum is conserved in an isolated system
$$
p_{1i}+p_{2i}=p_{1f}+p_{2f}
$$
- Kinetic energy is conserved
$$
K_{1i}+K_{2i}=K_{1f}+K_{2f}
$$
