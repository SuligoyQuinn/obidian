## Interaction Pairs
- forces always come in pairs: when two objects interact, each object exerts a force on the other
- Force vector can be written as $\vec{F}_{agentOnForce}$
## Newtons 3rd law
If object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equal magnitude force on object A in the opposite direction
### Example
- When a hammer hits a nail, it exerts a forward force on the nail
- At the same time the nail exerts a backwards force on the hammer
### Not possible
- interaction force pairs cannot act on the same object
- In book example, both forces act on book
## Newton's Laws Summary
1. Law of Inertia
2. Sum of forces cause acceleration
3. Equal and opposite forces
## Gravitational Force
- The pull of a planet on an object towards its center
- Long range force
- always vertically downwards
$$
\vec{F}_{g}=mg
$$
- gravitational acceleration $|\vec{a}_{g}|=g=9.8m/s^2$
## Mass vs Weight
- The mass of an object measures how much matter it has (measured in kilograms)
- the weight of an object is the measurement of "weighing" an object
	- weight is due to the spring force acting on the object
	- since the object isn't directly on the spring, the weight would usually be the normal force  
## Translational Equilibrium
- when an object is at rest or moving at a constant velocity, the vector sum of the forces is exerted on the object is zero, $\sum \vec{F}=0\vec{}$, and the object is said to be at equilibrium
